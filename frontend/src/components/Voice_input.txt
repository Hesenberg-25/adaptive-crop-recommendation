===========       Feature No 7  Voice Input (Web Speech API)         ===========
The Problem: Rural users might struggle with complex sliders and text inputs.
The Solution: 
Many farmers may not want to fiddle with sliders and number inputs, or may not know exact numbers off the top of their head. So instead of typing "Nitrogen: 90, Temperature: 38°C," they can just tap a mic and say something like:

"My soil has high nitrogen and it's really hot today."

The app figures out the rest.

Q . How it works, in plain steps
Farmer taps the mic button. The browser (using its built-in speech recognition, like Chrome's voice typing) 
listens and converts speech to text in real time — no server needed for this part, it's free and instant.

Farmer taps "Use these values." The spoken sentence gets sent to our backend.
An AI model (Llama 3.3, via Groq) interprets the sentence. It's smart enough to understand vague, human language — "high nitrogen," "pretty hot,"
"light rain" — and convert it into actual numeric values the app's crop-recommendation model needs (like N=90 mg/kg, temperature=38°C). 
It only fills in values for things the farmer actually mentioned — it doesn't guess or invent data for anything unsaid.
The values auto-fill the form, and the app tells the farmer what it understood, e.g. "Got it — high nitrogen and hot conditions recorded."
If it fails or nothing was understood, the app doesn't crash — it just asks the farmer to try again or use the sliders manually.


Why this matters (the pitch angle)
Accessibility: Removes a literacy/tech-comfort barrier — many farmers are more comfortable speaking than typing precise numbers into a form.
Speed: One sentence replaces filling out 4-5 separate input fields.
Robustness: It's built to fail gracefully — unsupported browsers just hide the feature instead of breaking, and unclear speech gives a helpful error 
instead of garbage data.
Combines two AI layers smartly: free, on-device speech recognition for converting audio → text, and a cloud LLM only for the harder job of interpreting
 meaning → structured data — which keeps it fast and cheap rather than sending raw audio to a server.
